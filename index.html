Welcome

Axo's Film Maker v2

An animation, camera and cutscene tool built for Studio Lite. Pose a rig, set keys on a timeline, fly a camera, add shake and events, then export it or play it back in-game.

That little timeline above is a toy version of the real one. Click the ruler, press play, click a diamond. If it feels familiar, you're already halfway there.

What it does

  • Poses anything. Real rigs with Motor6D joints, or a pile of loose parts. Move and rotate with a gizmo, or use IK so a hand stays where you put it.
  • A proper timeline. Diamonds for keyframes, a summary track, zoom, a view bar, copy and paste, undo, snapping to your FPS.
  • Easing you can see. Sine, Quad, Cubic, Elastic, Bounce and friends, plus a drag-the-handles Bezier editor.
  • A real camera. Key a camera track, look through it, check it in a 16:9 preview window, and layer on shake, roll and FOV punches.
  • Animate any property. Lights, colors, transparency, Lighting, post effects, GUI, sounds. If it has a value, you can put a key on it.
  • Physics baking. Let a ragdoll fall for real, then turn the result into keyframes.
  • Two ways out. Standard Roblox KeyframeSequence objects, or a single "film" that the tiny AxoFilmPlayer script plays for you.

Install and set up

Film Maker v2 is made of several LocalScripts that cooperate. They share one table (_G.FilmMaker), and each one waits politely for the one before it, so the order you add them in doesn't matter.

Putting it together

  1. Make a ScreenGui.Put a ScreenGui where your GUI lives (for example StarterGui) and give it any name you like.
  2. Add a LocalScript per part.Create one LocalScript inside that ScreenGui for each of Part 1, 2, 3, 4, Physics and Preview, and paste the matching code in. Names don't matter.
  3. Press play.The title appears top-left, a status line shows hints under it, and the Timeline window opens along the bottom with the bar below it.

Part 1 reads script.Parent to find the ScreenGui. If its parent isn't a ScreenGui it quietly makes its own, FilmMakerGui, inside PlayerGui, so the others still find everything through _G.FilmMaker.

Heads upFilm Maker takes over your camera (it sets it to Scriptable) and anchors the parts of any rig you load. Both are put back when you remove the rig or close the tool with the red X at the end of the bottom bar.
Films onlyAxoFilmPlayer plays films and nothing else. If you exported a KeyframeSequence (the plain EXPORT button), play it with Roblox's KeyframeSequencePlayer instead.

The player (only for games that play films)

Put the AxoFilmPlayer code in a LocalScript that runs where films should play, for example inside StarterPlayerScripts. It publishes itself as _G.AxoFilmPlayer and sets .Ready = true. Details are in Playing films in your game.

If a part says it didn't load

Each part waits up to 30 seconds for the previous one (40 for Physics and Preview). If it gives up you'll see a warning in the Output, like "Film Maker part 2: part 1 did not load". That almost always means the earlier script errored or is missing. Fix that one first. More in Troubleshooting.

Your first animation

Let's make a model wave its arm, hold it, and put it back down. It takes about five minutes, and it covers ninety percent of how you'll use the tool.

  1. Load a rig.Click Load Rig in the bottom bar, then click your model in the world. The status line says Loaded rig: Name (N tracks), the camera swoops over to it, and each bone gets a track. Rigs are the way to go for everything, loose parts included (group them in a Model first).
  2. Get your bearings.Hold the middle mouse button to orbit, scroll to zoom, hold the right mouse button and use WASDQE to fly. On a phone, drag to move the camera and pinch with two fingers.
  3. Lock in a starting pose.Open the Properties window and press Key Rig while the playhead is at 0. That puts a key on every bone, so the rig has a clean "before".
  4. Move the playhead.Click on the ruler at about one second. The yellow line follows you.
  5. Pose a bone.Click the upper arm in the world (or its row in the timeline). Press R for rotate and drag one of the orange arcs. Because Auto-Key: On is lit, a diamond appears on that track the moment you let go.
  6. Do it again later.Click the ruler at two seconds and pose the arm a bit higher, at three seconds put it back to the start. Tip: copy and paste a pose with Copy Rig Pose and Paste Rig Pose.
  7. Watch it.Press Space. The timeline loops by default. Press Space again to stop, or step one frame at a time with the arrow keys.
  8. Give it some weight.Click a diamond (or box-select several), open Easing and try Quad + InOut. Notice how the arm eases instead of sliding.
  9. Trim to fit.The default length is 10 seconds. Press Trim End in the timeline to snap the length to your last key.
  10. Export.Press the green EXPORT button. A KeyframeSequence appears under workspace.CurrentCamera. Want camera work, shake and property animation too? Use Export Film instead.
TipMade a mess? Ctrl+Z undoes it (up to 60 steps), Ctrl+Y or Ctrl+Shift+Z redoes it.
Good to knowThere's no save button. Your project lives in memory while the tool is open. Export Film and Import Film are how you save and reload your work. See FAQ.

Interface tour

Title and status line

Top-left shows the title and a single line of text under it. That line is the tool's voice: it shows hints (like the shortcuts list) and, for five seconds after something happens, a message such as Camera keyed or Copied 3 key(s). When it's quiet it falls back to the hint.

The bottom bar

The strip along the bottom is your toolbox. It scrolls sideways when it runs out of room. Reading left to right, it's grouped like this:

  • Loading: Load Rig, Parts Folder (legacy)
  • Tools: Move (G), Rotate (R), Space: World, Snap: On, Mode: FK, IK - Chain: 2 IK +, Grab: End, Flip Bend

  • Touch only: Drag: Orbit, Multi-Select
  • Camera: Cam View, Key Cam
  • Windows: Timeline, Properties, Camera, Sequence, then Explorer, Object, Reset Objs, Easing, Export Film, Cam FX, Import Film, Physics, Preview, Edit Cam
  • Finish: Import, EXPORT, X

Buttons that are switches turn blue when they're on. See the full list in the bottom bar reference.

Rigs and bones

A rig is whatever you load. A bone is one animatable part of it, and every bone gets its own track in the timeline.

Loading a rig

  • Load Rig then click anything in the world. The tool walks up from the part you clicked to find the best model: the nearest one containing a Humanoid or a Motor6D, otherwise the nearest model, otherwise the part itself.
  • Parts Folder is a legacy button from the first version of Film Maker. It loads workspace.PartsFolder as one rig, and it's a clumsy way to handle rigs: everything is lumped together with no real structure. It's still there so old projects keep working, but for new work load a proper model with Load Rig (put loose parts in a Model if you need to).
  • Loading the same thing twice just selects the one you already have. You can load as many rigs as you want.

What becomes a bone

  • Every Motor6D whose two parts are both inside the rig becomes a joint bone. Children are indented under their parents in the timeline. The selection label says (joint, N keys).
  • A part that no Motor6D hangs from (like a root part or a loose prop) becomes a free / root bone. It moves the part itself, relative to where it started when you loaded it.
  • Parts joined to a bone with a Weld or WeldConstraint come along for the ride as followers. They don't get tracks of their own.
  • Bone track names are the part names.
Heads upLoading a rig anchors every part in it so it can be posed. The original anchored state and position are restored when you remove the rig.

Removing a rig

Each rig has a blue header row in the timeline reading [Rig] Name with a red Remove button. Removing restores every part's original Anchored value and position, resets all joint transforms, and clears its keys and the undo history. Click the header to select the rig (this is what Key Rig, Reset Rig and friends act on).

Copying a pose between rigs

Copy Rig Pose remembers every bone's pose by name. Paste Rig Pose applies it to the selected rig wherever the names match, and keys it at the playhead if Auto-Key is on. Handy for two copies of the same character.

Posing

Selecting a bone

Click a part of the rig in the world, or click the bone's name in the timeline. The selected bone gets a blue outline and the Properties window shows its name and key count.

The gizmo

  • Move (G) shows green arrows. Rotate (R) shows orange arcs. For joint bones, rotation pivots around the joint, which is usually what you want.
  • Space: World / Space: Local chooses whether the handles line up with the world or with the bone.
  • Snap: On rounds drags. The steps live in the Camera window under Gizmo Snap: Move (default 0.1 studs) and Rot (default 5 degrees). Set either to 0 to turn that one off.
  • The gizmo hides during playback and while nothing is selected.

Typing exact values

In the Properties window, the POSE section has Pos and Rot boxes (three each, rotation in degrees). Type, then click away or press Enter to commit. These are the bone's pose offset from its rest position, not world coordinates. If the camera track is selected, the boxes show the camera instead.

Resetting

  • Reset Pose sets the selected bone back to rest.
  • Reset Rig does it for every bone in the rig.
  • Both put a key at the playhead if Auto-Key is on.
Heads upA bone that has keys gets re-read from those keys every time the time changes. If you pose it with Auto-Key off and then scrub, your pose snaps back. Press Key (or I) to keep it.

IK: moving the hand, not the shoulder

Inverse kinematics lets you drag the end of a limb and have the rest follow. Turn it on with Mode: IK or press Tab.

  1. Select the end bone, such as the hand or foot. It needs to be a joint bone (one made from a Motor6D).
  2. Set how many bones should bend with IK - / IK +. Chain: 2 is the selected bone plus its parent, perfect for forearm and upper arm. The range is 1 to 8.
  3. Use the Move (G) tool and drag an arrow. The chain solves as you drag.

While IK is on you'll see a small orange ball on the end and a blue ball at the top of the chain, plus orange outlines on the chain's bones.

  • Grab: End (orange ball): the top of the chain stays put and the end follows your drag.
  • Grab: Top (pin end) (blue ball): the end stays pinned where it is and the body follows your drag. Great for a planted foot.
  • Click either ball in the world to switch between them, or use the Grab button.
  • Flip Bend makes the next drag bend the other way (knees and elbows). It reads the current bend direction, so it usually just works.

When you let go, every bone in the chain is keyed together (plus the top bone if you used the pin).

Under the hoodTwo-bone chains use a direct solution with a bend direction. Longer chains use an iterative solver (20 passes). IK only applies to the Move tool.

Keyframes

A key is a value saved at a moment in time. Between two keys the tool blends from one to the other using the easing you chose. Keys snap to whole frames of your FPS.

Making keys

HowWhat it does
Auto-Key: OnKeys the selected bone (or IK chain, or object) whenever you finish a gizmo drag, commit a pose number, reset, or paste a rig pose.
I or K, KeyKeys whatever track is selected, at the playhead. On a bone: its pose. On the camera: the current view. On an FX channel: the value in the Camera FX box. On a property: its current value. On Markers: a new marker.
Key Sel (I)Same as above, in the Properties window.
Key RigKeys every bone of the selected rig at the playhead. Use it for a clean starting pose.

Keys that land past the end of the timeline stretch the length to include them.

What happens outside your keys

  • Before the first key and after the last key, a bone holds its first or last pose.
  • A property track shows the original value until its first key.
  • A camera FX channel uses its default (no shake, no roll, FOV add 0, shake rate 14 Hz) until its first key.

Deleting

Press X or Delete. If diamonds are selected, those go. If nothing is selected, the key on the selected track exactly under the playhead goes.

Copy and paste

Select diamonds, press Ctrl+C (or Copy Keys), move the playhead, press Ctrl+V (or Paste Keys). The pasted keys keep their spacing, start at the playhead, land on the same tracks, and replace any key already there. The pasted keys end up selected.

Undo and redo

Ctrl+Z undoes, Ctrl+Y or Ctrl+Shift+Z redoes. There are Undo / Redo buttons too. It remembers the last 60 steps and covers bones, camera, markers, FX and property tracks. Loading or removing a rig clears the history, so finish thinking before you remove one.

The timeline

What you're looking at

  • Transport row across the top: playback, loop, time, length and FPS, plus quick key and zoom buttons.
  • Ruler with the yellow playhead. A red grip at the end marks the length, and everything past it is shaded.
  • View bar (labelled "view") just under the ruler. It's a minimap of the whole timeline.
  • Tracks, from top to bottom: Summary, Camera, Markers, any FX tracks, then one [Rig] group per rig (bones indented under parents) and one [Obj] group per animated object.

The time label reads like 1.25s F38 / 300: seconds, current frame, total frames. Past a minute it switches to minutes and seconds.

Getting around

  • Scrub: click or drag on the ruler, or click empty space on a track row.
  • Zoom: scroll the wheel over the ruler, or Ctrl+wheel anywhere in the timeline. On a phone, pinch with two fingers.
  • Pan: middle-mouse drag, Shift+wheel, or scroll the wheel over the view bar.
  • View bar: drag the bright block to pan, drag its edges to zoom, click elsewhere to jump there.
  • Set the length visually: drag the red grip on the ruler.

Selecting and moving keys

  • Click a diamond to select it. Hold Ctrl or Shift (or switch on Multi-Select on touch) to add or remove.
  • Box select: hold Ctrl or Shift, click empty space twice quickly, and drag on the second press. Everything inside the box is added.
  • Drag selected diamonds sideways to retime them. They snap to frames and never go below 0.
  • A diamond on the Summary track stands for everything keyed at that moment. Select or drag it to move all of them together.
  • Clicking a row's empty space selects that track and scrubs there.

When you click a diamond, the easing controls update to show that key's style, so you can see at a glance what it's using.

The Sequence window

Settings for the clip itself:

  • Name: what exports will be called.
  • Dur / FPS: same as the timeline boxes.
  • Spd: editor playback speed, from 0.05 to 8. It doesn't change the exported timing.
  • The priority button (Core, Idle, Movement, Action, Action2, Action3, Action4) sets the exported KeyframeSequence's animation priority. Click to cycle. Default is Action.
  • The Event marker section edits markers.

Easing

Easing is the difference between a robot arm and a human one. It describes how a value travels from one key to the next: slowly out, fast in the middle, with a bounce at the end, whatever you like.

Try the curves

The graph is an approximation drawn in your browser. The real thing is computed by Roblox's own tween math inside the tool, so tiny differences are possible. The vertical range goes from -0.5 to 1.5 so you can see overshoot, just like in the Easing window.

How a key's easing is read

Easing lives on the key that you're arriving at. A key with Quad / Out shapes the motion from the previous key up to itself. This matches how Roblox poses work. The info line in the Easing window says it too: "Segment into key at 1.5s".

Applying it

  • Select diamonds, then pick a style or direction. They all update at once.
  • Nothing selected? You're setting the default for new keys.
  • The Properties window has quick Style: / Dir: buttons that cycle through the list (right-click cycles backward).

What "baked" meansRoblox only understands a handful of easing styles natively. For the rest, EXPORT inserts an extra linear key for every frame in that stretch so it looks right. You get the same motion with more keys. Export Film keeps the real style names and Bezier numbers, and the player evaluates them exactly.

The Bezier editor

Pick Bezier in the Easing window. Two handles appear on the graph: drag them, or type the four numbers (x1 y1 x2 y2) in the boxes. X stays between 0 and 1, but Y can go from -0.5 to 1.5 for overshoot and anticipation.

Event markers

Markers are little purple flags on the Markers track. They don't move anything. They tell your game something happened: a footstep, a line of dialogue, a door slamming, the camera cut.

Adding and editing

  • Select the Markers track and press I, or open the Sequence window and press Add Marker At Playhead.
  • Click a marker's diamond, then edit its Name and Value in the Sequence window. Both are plain text.
  • Move, copy, paste and delete them like any other key. You can stack several at the same time.

Hearing them in your game

  • Exported KeyframeSequences carry them as standard KeyframeMarkers, so AnimationTrack:GetMarkerReachedSignal("Footstep") works as usual.
  • With AxoFilmPlayer, pass an onMarker function. It receives the name, the value, the time and the playback handle. See the player.
TipUse the marker Name for the kind of event and the Value for details, like Sound / Door_Slam. Your handler can then switch on one and use the other.

The camera

There are really two cameras. Your editor view is how you look around. The film camera is the one you animate on the Camera track. Keep them straight and everything else makes sense.

Looking around (editor view)

  • Orbit: hold the middle mouse button and drag.
  • Pan: Shift + middle mouse drag.
  • Zoom / dolly: scroll the wheel, or Ctrl + middle mouse drag.
  • Fly: hold the right mouse button and use WASD to move, E up, Q down. Hold Shift to go three times faster. Scroll while holding the button to change speed.
  • Focus: F centers the selected bone.
  • Views: the Camera window has Front, Right, Back and Top. The numpad does 1 front, 3 right, 7 top, and Ctrl flips to the opposite side.

Keying a shot

  1. Frame it.Fly to where you want the camera at this moment. Zoom and FOV in the Camera window if needed (1 to 120).
  2. Key it.Press Key Cam in the bar (or Key Camera in the Camera window, or select the Camera track and press I). A blue diamond appears on the Camera track.
  3. Move on.Go to another time, fly somewhere else, key again. The camera glides between them.

The Preview window also has Cam = View, which copies your current editor view onto the film camera at the playhead.

Looking through the film camera

Cam View (also Look Through Track in the Camera window) makes the screen show the film camera, with shake and roll applied. Pressing Play with camera keys does this automatically. The moment you orbit, fly or zoom, it switches back to your editor view. You'll need at least one camera key first, or you'll see "Key the camera first".

Edit Cam: moving the film camera with the gizmo

Turn on Edit Cam and the Move and Rotate gizmos grab the film camera instead of a bone. Great for small nudges. Each change keys the camera at the playhead if the camera already has keys or Auto-Key is on.

Seeing the camera in the 3D view

  • An orange wireframe frustum shows where the film camera is and what it sees. Toggle with Frustum in the Preview window.
  • A blue path line shows where the camera travels, with a yellow ball at every key. It needs at least two keys. Toggle with Path.

Camera FX

Effects sit on top of the camera track, so you animate the shot first and then add feeling. You'll only see them when you're looking through the film camera (Cam View) or in the Preview window.

Presets are placed at the playhead, then you can drag them around like any keys.

Animating objects and properties

Characters are only half of a scene. Doors open, lights flicker, the sun sets, a part fades out. For that you track an object and put keys on its properties.

The Explorer window

  • It lists the children of Workspace. Press the root button to switch to Lighting (that's where Atmosphere, Bloom, Blur, ColorCorrection and so on live).
  • Expand with +/-. Click a name to select it. Refresh and Collapse do what they say.
  • Type in the search box and press Enter to search by name (it shows the first 250 matches; the tree is capped at 250 rows too).
  • The button on the right of each row tracks (+) or untracks (-) the object. Tracked ones show a * before the name.
  • Things belonging to a loaded rig are dimmed, since the rig tools handle them.
TipWith Explorer or Object open, clicking something in the 3D world selects it in the Explorer. With neither open, clicking an object that isn't part of a rig just deselects.

The Object window

Shows the selected instance's common properties for its class. Each row has the name, an editor, a K button to key the current value at the playhead, and (once tracked) an x button to remove that property's track.

  • Track, Key All, Reset and Untrack sit across the top.
  • Booleans toggle on click. Enums have a > button to cycle. Instance properties let you click the value and then click a target in Explorer or the world (the nil button clears it).
  • Not in the list? Type any property name in add any property by name and press Add.

How property tracks behave

  • With Auto-Key on, dragging the gizmo on a tracked or untracked part/model creates a Pivot track and keys it. (Attachments use WorldCFrame.) Editing a value in the Object window does the same.
  • Numbers, Vector3, Vector2, Color3, CFrame, UDim2, UDim and NumberRange blend between keys using your easing.
  • Everything else (booleans, strings, enums, BrickColor, sequences, instances) holds its value until the next key, then jumps.
  • Before its first key a property shows its original value. Reset Objs puts all tracked objects back to their original values at any time. Closing the tool does it too.
  • Property tracks show up under an [Obj] header with a red Remove button, which untracks the object and restores it.

Physics baking

Some motion is a pain to hand-key: a body flopping down stairs, a crate tumbling off a ledge. The Physics window lets Roblox's real physics do it, then records the result as keyframes you can edit.

Baking

  1. Set the moment.Move the playhead to where the physics should start. The rig is posed as it is at that time.
  2. Choose a launch.Try a preset (Drop, Knock Back, Hurl, Launch Up, Tumble) or type your own launch and spin. Directions are relative to which way the target faces.
  3. Press Bake Physics.A progress bar fills while a hidden copy of your target is simulated in the real world. Your original is hidden meanwhile.
  4. Wait or stop.It ends by itself when the duration is reached or when everything's been still for a moment, or press Stop & Keep. Cancel throws it away.
  5. Play it.The status says how many frames were baked. Press Play to watch.

Exporting to KeyframeSequence

The green EXPORT button makes standard Roblox KeyframeSequence objects, the same kind the Animation Editor makes. Use this when you want a normal animation you can load and play on a character with the usual Animator tools.

What you get

Everything lands inside workspace.CurrentCamera, and old ones with the same name are replaced.

NameContains
Name or Name_RigNameOne sequence per rig that has keys. With a single rig it's just the sequence name; with several, each gets its rig's name added.
Name_CameraThe camera track: a pose called Camera, with the FOV stored as the keyframe's FieldOfView attribute.
Name_EventsProperty-track keys encoded as FM_Set markers, if you've animated any objects.
  • Event markers from your Markers track are written into every sequence.
  • The sequence's loop setting follows Loop, and its priority follows the Sequence window button.
  • Non-native easing is baked (see Easing).
  • Status: "Exported to workspace.CurrentCamera: ..." or "Nothing to export: no keyframes yet".
Why CurrentCamera?It's on purpose. Studio Lite clears out most of what you create while testing, but anything in CurrentCamera becomes a persistent instance that doesn't get cleared. That makes the camera the safe place for your exports: they're still there after the test ends, and they stay out of your place's own hierarchy.
TipFor object animation and camera FX, prefer films. They hold everything in one piece, and the player does the rest.

Importing a sequence

Import reads a KeyframeSequence into your timeline. First, load the rig it's for, because poses are matched to bone names (case doesn't matter). It looks in this order and uses the first thing it finds:

  1. The KeyframeSequence you've selected in Explorer.
  2. Sequences in CurrentCamera named after your Sequence name (or Name_...).
  3. Any other KeyframeSequence in CurrentCamera.
  4. The first one found in Workspace or ReplicatedStorage.

It replaces all existing keys, camera keys and markers (undoable), adopts the sequence's loop setting, and extends the length if needed. Poses named Camera become camera keys with their FieldOfView. Unknown easing styles become Cubic. Pose names that don't match any bone are listed in the Output as unmatched pose names.

Films: Export Film and Import Film

A film is your entire scene in one piece of text: rig animation, the camera track, camera FX, property tracks, markers, length, FPS and loop. It's the format AxoFilmPlayer plays, and it's also the closest thing the tool has to a save file.

Exporting a film

Press Export Film. It creates a StringValue named AxoFilm_YourSequenceName in workspace.CurrentCamera, holding the film as JSON. If your environment offers setclipboard it also copies the text to your clipboard, and if it offers writefile it saves AxoFilm_Name.json. The status line tells you which of those happened and how big the film is.

It sticks aroundThe StringValue is placed in CurrentCamera so it becomes a persistent instance and Studio Lite won't clear it. To play the film in a game, copy its Value text into a StringValue in ReplicatedStorage named AxoFilm_YourName (or into a StringValue named YourName inside a folder called AxoFilms in ReplicatedStorage).

What a film remembers

  • Rigs: only bones that have keys, by path from the rig, with an indication of whether they're joints.
  • Camera and FX: every key, with easing style, direction and Bezier numbers intact.
  • Objects: each tracked object's path and property keys. Number, boolean, string, Color3, Vector3, Vector2, CFrame, UDim2, UDim, NumberRange, BrickColor, enums and instance references are supported. A NumberSequence or ColorSequence is simplified to its first value.
  • Markers: time, name and value.

Importing a film

Import Film reads a film back in, replacing the camera, FX, marker, bone and object keys. It finds the film in this order: a StringValue you've selected in Explorer, then AxoFilm_YourName in the camera, then any AxoFilm_* StringValue in the camera or ReplicatedStorage.

  • Rigs in the film that aren't loaded yet are loaded for you by path, if they exist in the game.
  • Objects are tracked by path, and their tracks restored.
  • Sequence name, FPS, length and loop are adopted.
  • The status line reports how many rig tracks and properties were imported and how many rig tracks were skipped (because their rig couldn't be found). Load that rig and import again.

Playing films in your game

AxoFilmPlayer is a small, self-contained script. It plays a film: moves the rig bones, drives the camera, applies FX, changes object properties and fires your markers. It has no UI and doesn't need Film Maker at all.

Films onlyAxoFilmPlayer plays films and nothing else. If you exported a KeyframeSequence (the plain EXPORT button), play it with Roblox's KeyframeSequencePlayer instead.

Quick start

Run the player in a LocalScript, put your film in ReplicatedStorage, and call Play from another LocalScript.

repeat task.wait() until _G.AxoFilmPlayer and _G.AxoFilmPlayer.Ready

local Player = _G.AxoFilmPlayer
local handle = Player.Play("Intro")
Player.Wait(handle)
print("cutscene done")

Where films are found

A name like "Intro" is looked up, in order, as ReplicatedStorage.AxoFilm_Intro, then CurrentCamera.AxoFilm_Intro, then inside ReplicatedStorage.AxoFilms as either Intro or AxoFilm_Intro.

You can also pass the StringValue itself, a JSON string (anything starting with {), or an already decoded table. Loaded films are cached, and reloaded if the StringValue's text changes.

Options

Player.Play(source, options) takes an optional table:

OptionDefaultMeaning
speed1Playback speed multiplier.
loopFrom the filmForce looping on or off.
startTime0Start part-way in, in seconds.
cameratrueSet to false to not touch the camera at all.
restoreCameratrueSet to false to leave the camera where the film ended.
restoreWorldfalsePut bones and properties back to how they were when playback ends.
rigs-A table mapping rig name to a different instance, to reuse an animation on another model.
objs-A table mapping an object's path (joined with /, like "Workspace/Door") to a different instance.
onMarker-Function called as (name, value, time, handle) when a marker is reached.
onEnd-Function called with the handle when playback ends.
 
local handle = Player.Play("Intro", {
    speed = 1,
    loop = false,
    restoreWorld = true,
    rigs = { Knight = workspace.KnightClone },
    onMarker = function(name, value, time, h)
        if name == "Sound" then
            print("play", value, "at", time)
        end
    end,
    onEnd = function(h)
        print("film ended")
    end,
})

Controlling playback

Play returns a handle (or nil with a warning if the film couldn't be loaded). Call its methods with a colon.

MemberWhat it is
handle.TimeCurrent time in seconds.
handle.Playing, handle.EndedState flags.
handle.Speed, handle.LoopCurrent settings.
handle.Duration, handle.FilmLength in seconds, and the decoded film table.
handle:Pause() handle:Resume()Pause and resume.
handle:SetSpeed(n)Change speed.
handle:Seek(t)Jump to a time (clamped to the film) and show that frame.
handle:Stop(restoreWorld)End it now. Pass true to put bones and properties back.
handle:Reset()Put bones and properties back without stopping.

And on the player itself: Player.Wait(handle) yields until a handle ends, Player.StopAll(restoreWorld) stops everything, Player.Active lists running handles, Player.Get(name) returns the StringValue, and Player.Load(source) returns the decoded film (or nil and a reason).

Things worth knowing

  • The camera is set to Scriptable while a film with camera or FX data is playing, then restored on end (unless you turned that off).
  • Markers fire once as the time crosses them, including the very end. When a film loops, they fire again each lap.
  • The player moves joints through Motor6D.Transform, and moves free bones by setting the part's CFrame. Keep those root parts anchored so physics doesn't fight the animation.
  • Bones, rigs or objects the film refers to but can't find are simply skipped. The rest still plays.

Recipes

A cutscene that waits, then gives control back

local Player = _G.AxoFilmPlayer

local function playCutscene(name)
    local handle = Player.Play(name, { restoreWorld = true })
    if handle then
        Player.Wait(handle)
    end
end

Common problems

My pose keeps snapping back when I scrub

You edited with Auto-Key off, so the pose was never saved. Press Key (I) after you pose, or turn Auto-Key on.

Clicking in the world doesn't select my rig

Clicks on the UI don't pass through, so check you aren't clicking a window. Also the part must belong to a loaded rig. If it doesn't, open the Explorer to pick ordinary objects instead.

No gizmo shows up

It hides while playing, and it needs a selected bone (or a selected object that isn't part of a rig). Press Space to stop, then click the bone.

IK doesn't move anything

IK works on joint bones (from Motor6Ds) and with the Move tool. A free or root bone can't be solved. Select the end joint, and raise Chain if the limb is longer.

The view is stuck on the film camera

Orbit, fly or zoom to return to your editor view, or toggle Cam View off.

My export doesn't match the editor

Check that FPS and length match what you expect, that non-native easing was baked, and that the camera FX aren't in a plain KeyframeSequence. For a faithful copy of everything, use a film.

The Preview is empty or missing things

Press Rebuild. Terrain isn't shown, and very large scenes are cut off, so try Scope: Objects.

Nothing happens when I press a key

Shortcuts are paused while a text box has focus. Click on the 3D view first.

FAQ

Is there a Save button?

No. Your project lives in memory while the tool is open. Export Film gives you the entire scene as text you can keep, and Import Film loads it back. Do that before you close the game.

Can I animate more than one character at once?

Yes. Load as many rigs as you like. Each gets its own header and tracks, and exports separately.

Does it work on mobile?

Yes. It detects touch devices and gives you larger buttons, drag-to-orbit, pinch and a Multi-Select toggle. See Touch.

Does it work on Humanoid characters (R6, R15)?

It works on anything made of parts. Motor6D joints (which R6 and R15 rigs use) become joint bones automatically. The Humanoid isn't required.

Does it run on the server?

No, the editor is a set of LocalScripts and works with the client's camera and GUI. The player is client-side too. If you need other players to see a cutscene, trigger it on each client.

Why are some of my parts not on the timeline?

Parts welded to a bone follow it and don't get their own track. Unwelded parts get a free track each.

Why does Export create extra keys?

That's easing being baked for styles Roblox doesn't support natively. See Styles and directions.

Can AxoFilmPlayer play my KeyframeSequence?

No. It is made only for films (Export Film). For KeyframeSequences, use KeyframeSequencePlayer.

Can I use the film player without Film Maker?

Yes. The player is separate, and only needs the film.

Why does everything export into CurrentCamera?

Because anything placed in CurrentCamera becomes a persistent instance, so Studio Lite doesn't clear it. That's how your exports survive. See Exporting.

Should I use the Parts Folder button?

No. It's a legacy leftover from the first version and a rough way to handle rigs. Use Load Rig on a model instead.

Where's the camera I exported?

In workspace.CurrentCamera, as Name_Camera. See Exporting.

What's next (psst)

  • A standalone game. Film Maker is being planned as its own game that exports easily over to Studio Lite. Working outside Studio Lite means its limitations stop getting in the way, so there's a lot more room for flexibility.
  • Collaboration. Working on animations together is on the wishlist, and a standalone game is what makes it possible.
